# Framework — Sustainability Paper (Earth, Religion & Organisations)

**Phase:** M3–4 (co-authored alongside Framework v1 publication)
**Status:** Planned

---

## Purpose

Define the requirements for the companion academic paper that extends the Kokoro project from interpersonal cultural translation to organisational-level sustainability. The paper argues that religion is a wisdom source — not a compliance risk — and that organisations which learn the religion and culture of the places they touch can become trustees of nature and society rather than extractors of them.

This document specifies what the paper must argue, what it must evidence, and what it must deliver as an academic and practical output.

## Central Thesis

> "An organisation that learns the religion and culture of the places it touches — and acts from kokoro — becomes a trustee of nature and society, not an extractor of them."

The paper must demonstrate this thesis through three moves:

1. **Learn** — Show that religion encodes long-tested wisdom on duty, restraint, community, and reverence for nature; argue it belongs in the sustainability conversation
2. **Act** — Present the five-pillar Kokoro Framework as a concrete, measurable path from compliance to conscience
3. **Endure** — Show that trust earned over time, grounded in cultural humility, outlasts any technology or programme

## Functional Requirements

### Argument Structure

**FR-SUP-001:** The paper must open by establishing the gap: most sustainability frameworks (ESG, GRI, IFRS S2) treat religion and culture as risks to manage. The paper argues they are wisdom sources to engage.

**FR-SUP-002:** The paper must present the convergence of five wisdom traditions as evidence that cross-cultural ethical alignment is possible:

| Tradition | Core contribution to sustainability ethics |
|---|---|
| Shinto (神道) | Kami in all things — forests, rivers, mountains are sacred; reverence for place is stewardship's starting point |
| Buddhism (仏教) | Interbeing (緣起) — no self separable from world; harm to river is harm to self |
| Christianity | Integral ecology (Laudato Si') — cry of Earth and cry of the poor are one |
| Islam | Khilāfa (stewardship, not ownership) — humans are trustees; mīzān (balance) and ʿadl (justice) order market and ecology |
| Indigenous / Animist | Land as ancestor, place as kin — relationship, not ownership, is the moral primitive |

**FR-SUP-003:** The paper must draw a clear distinction between "respect" (compliance floor) and "kokoro" (conscience-driven action):

| Dimension | Respect (compliance) | Kokoro (conscience) |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation | Don't offend | Act because the other matters |
| Religious engagement | Avoid difficult topics | Engage with humility |
| Treatment of religion | Legal risk | Moral source |
| Measurement | Complaints avoided | Trust earned |

**FR-SUP-004:** The paper must present the five-pillar Kokoro Framework as the operational model for moving from respect to kokoro. Each pillar must be introduced with its cultural-religious grounding, not just its KPIs.

**FR-SUP-005:** The paper must include a three-horizon roadmap showing how organisations implement the framework over time:

| Horizon | Timeframe | Focus |
|---|---|---|
| H1: Learn | 0–12 months | Cultural-religious literacy, advisor hiring, baseline measurement |
| H2: Act | 12–36 months | Active KPI targets, FPIC + living-wage compliance, community councils |
| H3: Restore | 36+ months | Net-positive nature outcomes, recognised trustee status, framework open-sourced |

### Evidence & Grounding

**FR-SUP-006:** The paper must cite primary religious texts or authoritative secondary sources for each wisdom tradition it draws upon. Citations must meet Shizenkan academic standards.

**FR-SUP-007:** The paper must reference the Vnext pilot as a live example of the framework being tested. Pilot data referenced in the paper must be anonymised and consistent with the privacy requirements in `engine-privacy-and-data.md`.

**FR-SUP-008:** The paper must engage existing Religion & Sustainability literature. It must not present the Kokoro Framework as the first or only such framework; it must situate it within the field.

### Outputs

**FR-SUP-009:** The paper must be suitable for submission to a Religion & Sustainability academic venue. The Shizenkan advisor must confirm venue-readiness before submission.

**FR-SUP-010:** The paper must be co-authored. The cultural advisor and Shizenkan advisor must both be credited appropriately.

**FR-SUP-011:** A non-academic summary of the paper's argument (2–3 pages, plain language) must be prepared for inclusion in the board pitch and public playbook.

### Guardrails

**FR-SUP-012:** The paper must not treat any religious tradition as monolithic or fixed. All wisdom-tradition references must acknowledge internal diversity and avoid cultural essentialism.

**FR-SUP-013:** The paper must not position Vietnam or Japan as exotic wisdom sources subordinate to Western sustainability frameworks. It must treat them as equal contributors to a shared ethical conversation.

**FR-SUP-014:** Religious engagement in the paper must be positioned as a source of moral wisdom, not as a legal or compliance matter. Any framing that reduces religion to risk management contradicts the paper's thesis and must be revised.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The paper presents a coherent argument across all three moves (Learn, Act, Endure) without gaps
- All five wisdom traditions are represented with at least one primary or authoritative secondary source each
- The Kokoro vs. compliance distinction is clearly drawn and supported by evidence
- The three-horizon roadmap is consistent with the five-pillar framework KPIs in `framework-five-pillars.md`
- The Shizenkan advisor confirms the paper meets academic standards before M4 publication target
- A plain-language summary is complete and reviewed by M7

---

## Constraints

- All pilot data referenced must be anonymised in accordance with `engine-privacy-and-data.md`
- The paper must not make commercial claims about the Kokoro engine or Vnext
- Religious tradition descriptions must be reviewed by the cultural advisor before inclusion
